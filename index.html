THESIS PROJECT

ReColor — Mobile Vision Assistive App

Real-time color vision remapping for color blind users. Daltonization algorithms, CIE Lab color space — clinically validated with PERI. Submitted to Google Play Store.

BEST CS THESIS
BEST RESEARCH
Problem
About 350 million people live with color vision deficiency, including an estimated 5.17% of Filipino males, yet existing mobile tools are mostly single-function apps that do not combine screening, correction and awareness.
My role
Project lead: led end-to-end delivery from scoping and clinical validation to Google Play, managing scope, dependencies and timeline across developer, clinical and end-user workstreams.
Key decision
Set hue rotation as the baseline and let the metrics decide: our proposed daltonization algorithm had to outperform it on measured color discrimination before becoming the core method. It did, with a +6.91 delta-E gain.
Delivery
A React Native app with four modular image-processing services, validated by 30 end users, 10 IT experts and 3 clinical specialists from the Philippine Eye Research Institute (PERI).
Outcome
Daltonization confirmed as the core method, 100% color identification accuracy and a 3.92/4 clinical expert rating. Won Best CS Thesis and Best Research for Social Sciences & Humanities.
ABSTRACT

Color vision deficiency (CVD) affects approximately 350 million individuals worldwide, including an estimated 5.17% of Filipino males, predominantly with the deutan subtype. Despite its prevalence, existing mobile assistive tools are often limited to single-function applications that do not integrate screening, correction, and awareness. This study presents ReColor, an Android-based mobile vision assistive application designed to address this gap through a unified set of image processing techniques.

The system integrates four core components: digitized Ishihara screening for preliminary detection, LMS-based adaptive daltonization for color enhancement, CIELAB ΔE-based color identification, and GPU-accelerated CVD simulation. HSV-based hue rotation was implemented as a baseline against which the proposed daltonization method was evaluated. Additionally, a U-Net convolutional neural network was developed and evaluated as a research extension, but was not deployed in the final system due to computational constraints.

A mixed-methods evaluation was conducted with 30 general end users. Results show that daltonization outperformed the hue rotation baseline in color discrimination, while the color identification module achieved an accuracy of 100%. GPU-based simulation demonstrated high fidelity, with a mean ΔE of 0.025 compared to a double-precision CPU reference. System testing achieved a 100% pass rate across defined test cases, and user evaluation yielded a grand mean of 3.60 (Highly Acceptable) with strong internal consistency (Cronbach’s α = 0.9348).

The study was conducted under a Memorandum of Understanding with the Philippine Eye Research Institute (PERI), ensuring institutional support and alignment with national vision health initiatives under Republic Act No. 11358. By integrating screening, assistive enhancement, and awareness features into a single mobile platform, ReColor provides a practical, accessible, and scalable solution for supporting individuals with color vision deficiency and advancing inclusive technology in the Philippine context.

My role: Product Owner · PRD co-author · VeloBuild CEO

Problem

College of Computing Studies information is scattered across social media announcements, paper attendance sheets, ad-hoc Google Forms, and spreadsheets used to hand out certification exam keys.

Users · 3 roles

  • Students join orgs, register for events, show a personal QR ticket, receive certification keys
  • Org officers run org pages, events, attendance scanning and forms
  • Faculty admins govern orgs, announcements and certification key batches

Mobile appfor students and org officers
Web admin portalfor faculty, deployed separately

MVP features

Org hubMain orgs and sub-orgs with their own pages, posts and membership applications
Offline QR attendanceOfficers scan with zero connectivity; check-in, check-out and late status sync when back online
Forms & evaluationsConditional membership and post-event forms, with metrics for event hosts
Certification keysFaculty upload one CSV; registered students get the key in-app with a push alert

Roadmap · 4 sprints

  1. Sprint 1 · NextFoundation & security

Deliberately out of scope

  • Likes, comments and student chat

PROJECT LEAD · TEAM OF 4 · DATA SCIENCE COURSE PROJECT · DEC 2025

Sentinel: Malaria Hotspot Model

A baseline model of regional malaria cases in the Philippines, built on 10 years of Department of Health regional data.

XGBoost · Python
Problem
Malaria cases nearly doubled from 3,245 in 2022 to 6,248 in 2023, and surveillance mostly reacts to past case counts instead of pointing health teams to where risk is concentrated.
My role
Project lead of a 4-person team.
Key decision
Chose XGBoost after comparing it with Random Forest, Naive Bayes and a linear model, because it handles non-linear patterns and missing data best.
Finding
One region carries almost the entire signal: Region 4B, which includes Palawan, so outbreak resources belong there first.
What we learned
With only region and year as inputs, the model describes past outbreaks but cannot forecast: it returns the same values for every year after its training data. Next step: add lagged rainfall and temperature data and validate on held-out years.
